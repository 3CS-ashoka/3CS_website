Content is as follows: The Centre for Climate Change and Sustainability (3CS) at Ashoka University which was established in 2019 aims to bring together members of the Ashoka community with a shared interest in studying, communicating, and mitigating the effects of human-induced climate change.

In its first 6 years of existence, the 3CS established itself as one of Ashoka’s most vibrant, active, and multi-disciplinary centres. The inaugural 3CS-organized meeting in February 2021, ‘healing earth’ held in the immediate aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic, showcased the centre’s approach. It combined rigorous academic methodologies addressing climate change and its impacts, with approaches to sustainability that emphasized the broader aspects of ecological health. The Centre has also supported an outstanding array of research by Ashoka faculty on the subject. Building on the success of the 3CS, we now propose to give 3CS a new direction, dynamism, and energy. 

The Centre will serve as a hub for interdisciplinary research, innovation, and action on climate change and sustainability. Bringing together expertise from the natural sciences, social sciences, humanities, policy, business, and technology, the Centre will generate knowledge and solutions that address urgent sustainability challenges of local importance while contributing to broader international thought and practice. A defining focus of the Centre will be to strengthen climate research, risk assessment, adaptation planning, resource management, and public engagement, supported by responsible use of artificial intelligence and emerging technologies.

VISION

To create a leading research centre on climate and sustainability with the country’s most vibrant and sophisticated climate and sustainability communication program, which will bring together scientists, humanists, and social scientists, along with professionals and practitioners in the field.

OBJECTIVES

The Centre aims to pursue three core objectives: research, knowledge-to-action, and training and education.

1. Advance research on climate change and sustainability by developing a focused research agenda spanning climate science, adaptation, sustainable development, policy and governance, climate communication, and the role of AI and digital technologies in sustainability.
2. Translating Research into Sustainable Innovation and Policy Making by linking research and knowledge with communities to transform insights into practical solutions and public impact. The centre will aim to move promising ideas from research to real-world use, supporting start-ups, community projects, digital tools, and policy changes that deliver measurable social and environmental results.
3. Strengthen public understanding, training and education to motivate action by creating new ways to communicate the urgency and immediacy of climate change, while also building knowledge and capacity among students, practitioners, policymakers, and communities so that people feel empowered to act rather than overwhelmed by helplessness or fatalism.

1. Research on climate change and sustainability: 

At 3CS, research will be organised through interdisciplinary working groups that form the core of the centre.  Each group would bring together affiliated faculty and students to identify priority questions, convene focused discussions, and help define promising directions for future work. These groups would then grow into vibrant research communities that will seed student-led projects, create internship opportunities tied to each theme, and establish pathways for postdoctoral and early-career researchers to contribute to the centre’s mission. This research ecosystem would be further strengthened by a named annual prize recognizing outstanding work on environment and climate change, alongside targeted capstone stipends that enable students to pursue high-impact sustainability projects. In doing so, the working groups would not only catalyse collaborative research and stronger proposals for external funding, but also build the human capital, intellectual leadership, and long-term research capacity needed for 3CS to become a lasting hub of interdisciplinary scholarship. 

1.	Climate Adaptation, Biodiversity and Resilience (Ecosystem restoration for climate resilience): A core focus of this research vertical will be biodiversity monitoring and ecological research to guide and measure ecosystem recovery. Through this approach, ecosystem restoration can become a practical and measurable strategy for strengthening climate resilience, supporting biodiversity, and enhancing long-term carbon storage in India, beginning with the Himalayan montane ecosystem and the mangrove-dominated coastal margins. In the Himalayas, restoration will address the impacts of habitat fragmentation, land-use change, and shifting precipitation patterns by restoring native vegetation, stabilizing slopes, and reconnecting habitat corridors across elevational gradients. These interventions will help strengthen biodiversity, ecological stability, and landscape resilience. Parallelly, mangrove ecosystems along the Bay of Bengal, globally significant blue carbon reservoirs, offer a critical opportunity for climate adaptation and resilience. In mature systems, sediment organic carbon typically makes up ~3-5% of soil mass, with radiocarbon ages of ~4,000-5,000 years, reflecting long-term carbon removal. Restoring these ecosystems can strengthen coastal resilience while rebuilding ecosystem integrity and long-term carbon removal through mangrove restoration, tidal exchange, and sediment stabilization. Together, these efforts position biodiversity assessments and ecosystem restoration as core strategies across some of India’s most climate-sensitive regions.
2.	Climate, Food Systems, and Public Health (or Climate Change, Sustainability, and Livelihood Resilience in Agriculture and Health): This research area will examine how climate change is reshaping food systems, nutrition, disease patterns, and livelihoods. It will focus on the interconnected challenges of agricultural stress, food insecurity, heat exposure, water-related risks, and changing health outcomes, while identifying pathways for resilience and sustainable adaptation. By linking research across agriculture, health, sustainability, and livelihoods, the Centre will generate evidence to inform policy, strengthen community resilience, and support more equitable development pathways.
3.	Climate Change and Rise in Environmental Antimicrobial Resistance: This vertical will focus on understanding how climate change influences antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in soil and water systems, followed by devising strategies to mitigate the abundance of AMR. It will examine how climate change and environmental degradation accelerate the emergence, persistence, and spread of resistant microbes and genes across interconnected wildlife, livestock, human, soil, and water systems. Bacterial AMR was associated with an estimated 4.71 million deaths globally in 2021, including 1.14 million deaths directly attributable to resistance, while 42% of household wastewater was not safely treated in 2022, releasing an estimated 113 billion m³ into the environment and increasing contamination risks for soils and water bodies (Naghavi et al, Lancet 2024). By integrating research on climate stress, environmental monitoring, microbial ecology, and public health, and by exploring novel antibiotics comprised of antimicrobial peptides, which have evolved over approximately 2 billion years, the Centre will generate evidence and practical pathways to reduce environmental AMR and support more sustainable and equitable health and environmental outcomes.
4.	AI for climate tech and sustainability: 3CS will pioneer rigorous Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification (MRV) systems built on reproducible, traceable, and scalable data infrastructure for climate and nature metrics. This work will span three areas: AI-enabled remote MRV using satellite, ground, and geospatial data; improved methods for baselines, uncertainty, and additionality; and multi-metric MRV that goes beyond carbon to include biodiversity, water security, and ecosystem resilience. By integrating computational tools with ecological science and transparent data protocols, the Centre will help build credible, lower-cost, and scalable systems for climate action and finance. Alongside this, the Centre will also support the independent evaluation of emerging carbon removal approaches such as Enhanced Rock Weathering (ERW), with a focus on real-world performance, net carbon balance, and long-term permanence. In partnership with institutions such as IISc and the IITs, 3CS will help develop robust measurement standards and field-validation frameworks to ensure that these technologies are assessed on the basis of credible evidence, enabling more informed investment, policy, and deployment decisions.
5.	The Environment Humanities and Social Science (EHUSS) focuses on the impact of climate change on the lived experiences of individuals and groups. It recognises that not all knowledge can flow from experts. EHUSS aims to learn about and from innovative individual and community-based micro solutions to climate change. It focuses on bringing to the fore the experiences, voices, and knowledge of groups at the frontlines of climate change in India. EHUSS has two broad principles. One, it recognises that not all knowledge and innovation can flow from experts. We can and should learn from people and communities around us. Two, a key component of adaptation to climate change is culture. The EHUSS promotes thinking of we can (re)orient our culture of engaging towards nature to build a sustainable future. After all, if for some reason, we cannot innovate our way out of climate change, then adaptation through a profound transformation in our culture of consumption, production and sensibility towards the environment may be the only way out for humanity to survive. While EHUSS raises important questions, deliberates on long-term transformations, and seeks to make a softer and long-term impact on how we related to nature through research, communication, and education, the EHUSS also aims to collaborate with other verticals in 3CS and through 3CS with other centres at Ashoka to co-solve environmental challenges facing India.
6.	Climate policy, Finance & Governance: This vertical will centrally focus on policies, practices, and institutions pertaining to energy transitions in India with the aim of helping it achieve a sustainable and viable energy infrastructure. Energy transition policy has evolved to become the cornerstone of urgent global climate action. Multitudinous technologies, pathways, and instruments are being explored in response to local contexts and political economies. Simultaneously, energy policy needs to respond to the complexities emerging at multiple levels: deepening geopolitical tensions and climate change impacts, the complexities of domestic developmental demands and institutional pitfalls, and local demand-side energy use. Increasingly termed as complex and messy, energy transition policy is evidently witnessing varied social, economic, and political implications. Public acceptance, rent-seeking, institutional and fossil-fuel lock-in dynamics, behavioural shift, equity and justice demand novel areas of frameworks and research to inform a purposive and time bound energy transition. The Energy Policy studies vertical will bridge the evolving, multidimensional challenges of the energy transition in the global south. Building an interdisciplinary research team, the vertical will focus on policy facing and data-backed research for a timely, equitable, sustainable energy transition.

Energy, Mobility, and Natural Resource Governance (or Zero-Emission Transitions and Natural Resource Governance): This vertical, in collaboration with other existing centres, will focus on the transitions needed to move toward zero-emission energy systems while strengthening the sustainable governance of natural resources. Examining the intersections of energy use, mobility, infrastructure, land, water, and resource management, it will identify pathways that reduce emissions, support resilience, and advance social equity. By integrating research on policy, technology, infrastructure, and behaviour, the Centre will generate evidence and practical pathways for development that are more inclusive and aligned with long-term sustainability goals.

2. Translating Research into Sustainable Innovation and Policy Making

Through this, 3CS aims to bring climate knowledge to the public by making key ideas available in Indian languages and supporting student-led solutions for real-world sustainability challenges. Flagship projects include the Climate Translation Lab, which offers Indian-language translations and easy-to-understand explainers, and the Climate Impact Hackathon. The hackathon will bring together student teams to create AI-powered climate solutions, such as dashboards for managing water, air, land, and biodiversity, and campus projects that improve recycling, reduce waste, and track resources. 

Importantly, the centre aims to build robust industry-academia partnerships to facilitate the translation of fundamental discoveries into product development and scalable innovation pipelines. It seeks to serve as a collaborative bridge that links academia, industry, and societal benefits. While the centre will offer seed grants, mentorship, and access to translational expertise to support promising ideas suitable for developing prototypes and products, industry partners can co-design pilot projects, provide domain-specific mentorship, and invest in early-stage innovation efforts. 

Finally, the centre aims to translate knowledge into policy through engagement with national programs, research collaborations, and international partnerships (including WHO, the World Bank, and UNDP), ensuring sustained global impact.
