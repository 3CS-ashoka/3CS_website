1.	Climate Adaptation, Biodiversity and Resilience 
A core focus of this research vertical will be biodiversity monitoring and ecological research to guide and measure ecosystem recovery. Through this approach, ecosystem restoration can become a practical and measurable strategy for strengthening climate resilience, supporting biodiversity, and enhancing long-term carbon storage in India, beginning with the Himalayan montane ecosystem and the mangrove-dominated coastal margins. In the Himalayas, restoration will address the impacts of habitat fragmentation, land-use change, and shifting precipitation patterns by restoring native vegetation, stabilizing slopes, and reconnecting habitat corridors across elevational gradients. These interventions will help strengthen biodiversity, ecological stability, and landscape resilience. Parallelly, mangrove ecosystems along the Bay of Bengal, globally significant blue carbon reservoirs, offer a critical opportunity for climate adaptation and resilience. In mature systems, sediment organic carbon typically makes up ~3-5% of soil mass, with radiocarbon ages of ~4,000-5,000 years, reflecting long-term carbon removal. Restoring these ecosystems can strengthen coastal resilience while rebuilding ecosystem integrity and long-term carbon removal through mangrove restoration, tidal exchange, and sediment stabilization. Together, these efforts position biodiversity assessments and ecosystem restoration as core strategies across some of India’s most climate-sensitive regions.
Ongoing Projects: 
•	Impact of Climate Change on Small Mammal Communities and Risks of Spillover Infections: This long-term study uses molecular and ecological methods across habitat gradients to assess the potential for zoonotic spillovers, establish a monitoring regime for zoonotic diseases, and document the evolutionary consequences of climate change on biodiversity.
•	Quantifying Teak Age to Understand the Impact of Climate Stress: This research delves into the genetic underpinnings of plant ageing using the bisulfite method, a groundbreaking exploration that parallels human genome studies associated with ageing by focusing on the economically vital teak species.
•	Quantifying Forest Fires and Fire Understory to Understand Climate-Fire-Forest Composition Interaction: This study explores the intricate relationship between climate, fire, and forest composition, recognising the potential compromise of ecosystem functions in low-species diversity environments, such as monoculture plantations.
•	Shifting Fish and Fisheries: Examining Fishers and Seafood Trade Responses to Shifting Fish Distributions in India: Synthesise ecological, economic, and citizen-sourced information to quantify marine ecological changes from climate change on India's east and west coasts

Future Projects: 
•	Montane ecosystem resilience initiative: To establish a monitoring and restoration program in selected Himalayan montane landscapes to reconnect fragmented habitat corridors and stabilise erosion-prone slopes across different elevations. 
Mangrove Blue Carbon Restoration: To launch a coastal restoration effort in mangrove-dominated areas of the Bay of Bengal, focusing on mangrove replanting, tidal exchange rehabilitation, and sediment stabilisation to restore ecosystem integrity and climate resilience.

2.	Climate, Food Systems, and Public 

This research area will examine how climate change is reshaping food systems, nutrition, disease patterns, and livelihoods. It will focus on the interconnected challenges of agricultural stress, food insecurity, heat exposure, water-related risks, and changing health outcomes, while identifying pathways for resilience and sustainable adaptation. By linking research across agriculture, health, sustainability, and livelihoods, the Centre will generate evidence to inform policy, strengthen community resilience, and support more equitable development pathways.
Ongoing Projects: 
•	Seed Stories: This documentary explores the conservation of 1,000+ endangered heirloom rice varieties in the Niyamgiri mountains, Odisha.
•	Drivers of Crop Residue Burning and its Mitigation: This research project aims to comprehend the drivers behind CRB and propose effective mitigation strategies.
•	One Health and the Impact of Climate Change: This multidisciplinary, collaborative application uses a One Health framework to examine the interconnected disease burden and patterns of infection transmission across human, animal, and environmental reservoirs.
Future Projects: 
•	Climate-Resilient Food Systems: Assess how rising temperatures and changing rainfall patterns affect crop productivity across various landscapes
Nutrition shifts and health: To understand how changes in nutrition and food consumption patterns influence disease risk and health outcomes.

3.	Climate Change and Rise in Environmental Antimicrobial Resistance: This vertical will focus on understanding how climate change influences antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in soil and water systems, followed by devising strategies to mitigate the abundance of AMR. It will examine how climate change and environmental degradation accelerate the emergence, persistence, and spread of resistant microbes and genes across interconnected wildlife, livestock, human, soil, and water systems. Bacterial AMR was associated with an estimated 4.71 million deaths globally in 2021, including 1.14 million deaths directly attributable to resistance, while 42% of household wastewater was not safely treated in 2022, releasing an estimated 113 billion m³ into the environment and increasing contamination risks for soils and water bodies (Naghavi et al, Lancet 2024). By integrating research on climate stress, environmental monitoring, microbial ecology, and public health, and by exploring novel antibiotics comprised of antimicrobial peptides, which have evolved over approximately 2 billion years, the Centre will generate evidence and practical pathways to reduce environmental AMR and support more sustainable and equitable health and environmental outcomes.
Ongoing Projects: 
•	Climate-Driven AMR in Soil and Water Systems: To understand how climatic changes, wastewater contamination, and land degradation influence the emergence, persistence, and spread of antimicrobial resistance across soil and water environments.
•	Identification of novel antimicrobials against AMR using AI-assisted technologies 
Future Projects: 
•	Antimicrobial Peptide Discovery as Therapeutics & Mitigation against rising Environmental AMR with climate warming: Develop mitigation strategies to reduce environmental AMR by combining surveillance of drug-resistant microbes with the discovery of counteractive novel antimicrobial peptides and nature-based control approaches across diverse Indian ecosystems (e.g., mountains, forests, to coastal regions)

4.	AI for climate tech and sustainability

3CS will pioneer rigorous Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification (MRV) systems built on reproducible, traceable, and scalable data infrastructure for climate and nature metrics. This work will span three areas: AI-enabled remote MRV using satellite, ground, and geospatial data; improved methods for baselines, uncertainty, and additionality; and multi-metric MRV that goes beyond carbon to include biodiversity, water security, and ecosystem resilience. By integrating computational tools with ecological science and transparent data protocols, the Centre will help build credible, lower-cost, and scalable systems for climate action and finance. Alongside this, the Centre will also support the independent evaluation of emerging carbon removal approaches such as Enhanced Rock Weathering (ERW), with a focus on real-world performance, net carbon balance, and long-term permanence. In partnership with institutions such as IISc and the IITs, 3CS will help develop robust measurement standards and field-validation frameworks to ensure that these technologies are assessed on the basis of credible evidence, enabling more informed investment, policy, and deployment decisions.

Ongoing Projects: 

•	Cost-Effective Air Quality Monitoring with Decision Support: The project is developing a cost-effective, scalable, one-stop system for air quality monitoring and assessment, with decision-support capabilities.

Future Projects: 

•	AI-Based Environmental Monitoring: Develop a reliable and scalable system that integrates satellite imagery, field measurements, and geospatial data with artificial intelligence to monitor carbon, biodiversity, water security, and ecosystem recovery.
•	Carbon Removal Technologies: Create a field-based framework to test new carbon removal methods, starting with Enhanced Rock Weathering.

5.	The Environment Humanities and Social Science (EHUSS) 
EHUSS focuses on the impact of climate change on the lived experiences of individuals and groups. It recognises that not all knowledge can flow from experts. EHUSS aims to learn about and from innovative individual and community-based micro solutions to climate change. It focuses on bringing to the fore the experiences, voices, and knowledge of groups at the frontlines of climate change in India. EHUSS has two broad principles. One, it recognises that not all knowledge and innovation can flow from experts. We can and should learn from people and communities around us. Two, a key component of adaptation to climate change is culture. The EHUSS promotes thinking of we can (re)orient our culture of engaging towards nature to build a sustainable future. After all, if for some reason, we cannot innovate our way out of climate change, then adaptation through a profound transformation in our culture of consumption, production and sensibility towards the environment may be the only way out for humanity to survive. While EHUSS raises important questions, deliberates on long-term transformations, and seeks to make a softer and long-term impact on how we related to nature through research, communication, and education, the EHUSS also aims to collaborate with other verticals in 3CS and through 3CS with other centres at Ashoka to co-solve environmental challenges facing India.
Ongoing Projects: 
•	Female Legislators and Forest Conservation in India: This research project investigates the impact of female elected political representatives on environmental outcomes, specifically forest cover growth in India.
•	The Indian Plant Humanities: This project is creating an archive of thought on plant life by writers, artists, philosophers, and scientists from the Indian subcontinent over two millennia, and is exploring environmental humanities at the intersection of botany, culture, and sustainability.
•	Amphibious Lives: This project is creating an archive of thought on plant life by writers, artists, philosophers, and scientists from the Indian subcontinent over two millennia while exploring environmental humanities at the intersection of botany, culture, and sustainability.

Future Projects: 


•	Environment and conservation policies and practices at all levels of the governance from global to local.
•	Studies on community-based localised micro solutions to environmental problems with a focus on ecologically sensitive areas of India.
•	Research on the meaning and impact on changing cultures of engagement with the environment with a focus on hobby-based activities such as birding, hiking, mountaineering, and wildlife photography.
•	Real estate development and urban sustainability.


6.	Climate Policy, Finance & Governance

This vertical will centrally focus on policies, practices, and institutions pertaining to energy transitions in India with the aim of helping it achieve a sustainable and viable energy infrastructure. Energy transition policy has evolved to become the cornerstone of urgent global climate action. Multitudinous technologies, pathways, and instruments are being explored in response to local contexts and political economies. Simultaneously, energy policy needs to respond to the complexities emerging at multiple levels: deepening geopolitical tensions and climate change impacts, the complexities of domestic developmental demands and institutional pitfalls, and local demand-side energy use. Increasingly termed as complex and messy, energy transition policy is evidently witnessing varied social, economic, and political implications. Public acceptance, rent-seeking, institutional and fossil-fuel lock-in dynamics, behavioural shift, equity and justice demand novel areas of frameworks and research to inform a purposive and time bound energy transition. The Energy Policy studies vertical will bridge the evolving, multidimensional challenges of the energy transition in the global south. Building an interdisciplinary research team, the vertical will focus on policy facing and data-backed research for a timely, equitable, sustainable energy transition. 
7.	Energy, Mobility, and Natural Resource Governance

This vertical, in collaboration with other existing centres, will focus on the transitions needed to move toward zero-emission energy systems while strengthening the sustainable governance of natural resources. Examining the intersections of energy use, mobility, infrastructure, land, water, and resource management, it will identify pathways that reduce emissions, support resilience, and advance social equity. By integrating research on policy, technology, infrastructure, and behaviour, the Centre will generate evidence and practical pathways for development that are more inclusive and aligned with long-term sustainability goals.
